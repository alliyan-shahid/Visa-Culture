Study in USA

Why the USA

Find a university that fits how you want to learn.

The United States offers everything from community colleges and small liberal arts colleges to large public universities and private research institutions. Compare programs, academic support and total costs as you build your shortlist.

01

Flexible undergraduate degrees

Many colleges let you take courses across disciplines before choosing a major. Check when you must declare and whether your intended major has separate entry requirements.

02

Research and applied learning

Depending on your program, you can build experience through faculty research, labs, capstone projects and internships.

03

Community college transfer

Start with an associate degree and apply to transfer into a bachelor's program. Check transfer agreements and which credits count toward your major.

04

International student support

Look for an international student office, academic advising and orientation. Ask about housing support, campus employment guidance and career services.

Education in the USA

Plan your application and budget.

Requirements and deadlines vary by college, degree and intake. Start with the official program checklist and compare application, scholarship and enrollment deadlines. EducationUSA application guide

Admissions

Prepare your academic records

Undergraduate applicants generally submit secondary-school records, such as HSSC or A-level results. Colleges may also ask for essays and recommendations. Graduate applicants typically submit degree transcripts, references and a statement of purpose. Check translation and credential evaluation rules.

Testing

Check English and test requirements

Check which English proficiency tests your university accepts, its minimum scores and any waiver criteria. SAT or ACT policies vary for undergraduate admission. Graduate programs set their own GRE or GMAT policies.

Tuition

Compare annual tuition and fees

For 2026–27, published U.S. undergraduate tuition and fees averaged USD 32,730 at public four-year colleges for out-of-state students and USD 46,350 at private nonprofit four-year colleges. Use your school's international rate or graduate program quote for your budget. College Board pricing data

Funding

Look for scholarships and aid early

Ask colleges about merit scholarships, need-based aid for international students and separate deadlines. Graduate students can ask departments about fellowships and teaching or research assistantships that offer a stipend or tuition support. EducationUSA funding guide

Work and future direction

Connect your degree to a career.

Compare internship support, alumni networks and graduate outcomes. If you hold F-1 status, ask your designated school official about Curricular Practical Training (CPT) for eligible placements or Optional Practical Training (OPT) for work related to your degree. OPT can provide up to 12 months of training, with a 24-month extension for eligible STEM graduates. USCIS guidance on OPT

Student visa and arrival

Prepare for your F-1 visa and first weeks.

Most international students pursuing an academic degree in the U.S. apply for an F-1 visa after admission. Follow the instructions from your school and the U.S. embassy or consulate where you apply. U.S. Department of State student visa guide

AdmissionGet your I-20
VisaApply for F-1
ArrivalCheck in on campus
01

Get your Form I-20

Accept an offer from a school certified by the Student and Exchange Visitor Program (SEVP). Provide the financial evidence your school requests so it can issue your Form I-20.

02

Pay fees and submit your application

Pay the USD 350 I-901 SEVIS fee, submit Form DS-160 and follow the consulate's instructions for the USD 185 visa application fee and interview. Check for any additional fees that apply.

03

Gather documents for the interview

Prepare your passport, signed I-20, DS-160 confirmation and fee receipts. The consulate may ask for academic records, proof of funding and evidence that you intend to depart after your studies.

04

Plan your arrival

New F-1 students may enter up to 30 days before the program start date on Form I-20. Confirm housing, health insurance, orientation and school check-in dates before traveling.

05

Maintain your status

Follow your school's full-time enrollment rules. Eligible F-1 on-campus work is generally limited to 20 hours per week while school is in session. Consult your designated school official before taking off-campus work.
