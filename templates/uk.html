Study in the United Kingdom

Why the UK

Find a course that fits your next step.

Compare universities across England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland. Look at what you’ll study, how you’ll be assessed and what the full degree will cost.

01

Focused undergraduate degrees

Many bachelor’s degrees take three years in England, Wales and Northern Ireland. Scottish honours degrees often take four. Check the duration of your course, especially if it includes a placement or year abroad.

02

Taught and research postgraduate study

Many full-time taught master’s degrees take one year. Research degrees follow different timelines and may require a proposal or a conversation with a prospective supervisor.

03

Foundation and pathway programmes

If you need more subject or language preparation, check for an international foundation year or pre-master’s course. Confirm progression requirements and the full cost of the pathway.

04

Support for international students

Check what each university offers through its international student advisers, orientation, academic skills support and careers service.

Education in the UK

Plan your application and budget.

Most undergraduate applications go through UCAS. For postgraduate study, check the university’s application process. Deadlines vary by course, with earlier dates for some universities and professional programmes. UCAS

Admissions

Check your academic qualifications

Check how each university assesses Pakistani HSSC, A-levels or previous degrees. Subject prerequisites, references, interviews, admissions tests and portfolios depend on the course.

English

Confirm which evidence is accepted

Check the university’s accepted English tests, minimum scores and exemptions. The Student visa usually requires English evidence as well, and the way you can provide it depends on your course and sponsor. GOV.UK English requirements

Tuition

Compare international fees by course

The British Council gives indicative international tuition ranges of £11,400–£38,000 for undergraduate study and £9,000–£30,000 for postgraduate study. Confirm the exact fee and payment schedule with the university. British Council cost guide

Funding

Look for scholarships early

Compare university awards with schemes such as GREAT Scholarships for Pakistan and Chevening for eligible postgraduate applicants. Check participating universities, what the award covers and separate application deadlines. British Council scholarship guide

Work and future direction

Connect your degree to a career.

Compare placements, employer links and career support. Eligible graduates can apply from within the UK for a Graduate visa. It lasts two years for applications made by 31 December 2026 and 18 months from 1 January 2027. Eligible doctoral graduates receive three years. GOV.UK Graduate visa guide

Life in the UK

Compare living costs before choosing a city.

The British Council suggests budgeting around £1,300–£1,400 a month in London or £900–£1,300 elsewhere in the UK. Compare actual rent, bills, food and transport for your campus, and check the separate Student visa financial requirements. British Council living cost estimates

Student visa and arrival

Prepare for your Student visa and arrival.

Check current eligibility, fees and documents before applying. Your university’s international student team can help with questions about your course and sponsorship. GOV.UK Student visa guide

01

Get your CAS

Meet the conditions of your offer from a licensed student sponsor so it can issue your Confirmation of Acceptance for Studies (CAS).

02

Prepare your financial evidence

Where required, show funds for the course fees listed on your CAS and the applicable living-cost amount. Check the evidence rules and how long you must hold the funds. GOV.UK financial requirements

03

Apply and check your documents

Have your passport and CAS ready, and budget for the visa fee and immigration health surcharge. Check whether you need a tuberculosis certificate from an approved clinic or an ATAS certificate for your course. GOV.UK document checklist

04

Get ready to travel and enrol

Check the start date of your visa, follow the instructions for accessing your eVisa, and confirm your housing and enrolment arrangements before travelling.

05

Follow your work conditions

Some full-time degree students may work up to 20 hours per week during term time. Check the conditions of your own visa and your university’s term dates before accepting work.
